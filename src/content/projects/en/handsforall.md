  approach:
    heading: What We Did
    text: |-
      We built the system on one decision: the donor should be able to give on the screen that convinced them.

      So the donation form is not on a separate page but embedded in the opening screen of the homepage — amount, name, email, one button. The campaign's running total sits in the top bar, so no one has to decide without seeing where their contribution lands.

      The second decision was about language. Charity brands usually fall to one of two extremes: leading with suffering and pressing the donor through guilt, or turning corporate and keeping their distance. We placed the brand outside both — energetic, and hopeful — from the logo and the palette through to the photography and the tone of the content.
  sections:
    - heading: Logo Design
      text: |-
        We drew the emblem out of the word itself: the last two letters of "all" become two figures holding hands. No separate symbol was needed — the name and the mark are the same thing.

        The unbroken looping pattern that grows out of the mark became the second layer of the identity; on its own it works as a texture across every surface, from a backpack to a beach flag.

        We built the logo system for different grounds and scales: horizontal use, a compact two-line lockup, and the emblem alone. The app icon comes in blue and orange variants.
      images:

    - heading: Corporate Identity
      text: |-
        We applied the identity to the surfaces the organisation genuinely uses: letterhead, business cards, branded pens, event flags and the backpacks carried on field distributions.

        In the non-profit world an identity is not tested in the office but in the field. Applications like the backpack and the flag carry the brand past the donation box, to the place the aid actually arrives.

        This is where the looping pattern earns its place: even on surfaces too small to carry a legible logo, the brand stays recognisable.

    - heading: Website
      text: |-
        We built the homepage around the campaign. The opening screen carries whichever campaign is running — during Ramadan, "One Iftar, A Thousand Hopes" — and the donation form sits directly beside it.

        Aid areas are split into colour-coded cards: iftar, Ramadan food package, water well, zakat and orphan support. The donor is not clicking a general donation box, but the work they came to support.

        Completed work sits in its own band as cards of evidence: the education initiative, water wells, food packages, orphan support. Below that, a field photography gallery, the blog, newsletter sign-up and the logos of member organisations complete the case for trust.

    - heading: Social Media
      text: |-
        We mapped the feed directly onto the organisation's areas of work: water wells, education, orphan support, food packages, qurbani. The highlight groups on the profile carry the same split, so a visitor reaches the area they care about in one tap.

        One line of the content is tied to the religious calendar — explanatory posts on what an Adaq Qurban is, and posts for Laylat al-Qadr and Eid al-Adha. These are the moments when giving concentrates; a campaign is not built on those days, it is prepared for them.

        Post-Eid thank-you posts are part of the feed too. An account that reaches the donor not only when it needs something, but after they have given.

        The images come from our own field shoots rather than a stock library — a child drinking from a well for the first time, a family sitting down to eat, a food parcel passing from hand to hand.

        We went to the field. We filmed the water well openings, the food distributions and the iftar tables in Kenya ourselves — the photography and the video are both ours.

        In the non-profit world original field footage is the most valuable asset there is. A relief organisation described through stock photography is indistinguishable from one that does nothing; the difference is made by a frame taken at the well.

        The material did not stay in one place: the gallery on the website, the social feed and the printed materials all draw on the same shoots. The two films we cut for the Ramadan campaigns carry the appeal directly.
