  approach:
    heading: What We Did
    text: |-
      In aesthetic surgery the decisive choice is tone. A language of promised results makes a merchant of the surgeon; a distant clinical language leaves the patient's real question — what will happen to me? — unanswered. We built the whole brand between those two.

      That single decision repeats in every layer: a heart rather than a medical symbol in the emblem, green rather than clinical blue in the palette, curiosity satisfied rather than surgery promoted in the content, and the surgeon's own account rather than a scripted edit in the videos.

      The result is that the logo, the colour and the way the brand speaks all say the same thing — the patient does not meet four different brands.
  sections:
    - heading: Logo & Corporate Identity
      text: |-
        The emblem joins a heart with the letter "B": one mark carrying both the surname and the feeling aesthetic surgery actually promises — to feel well in your own skin. The handwritten surname turns it into a signature, making this the brand of a person rather than a clinic.

        We chose green over clinical blue deliberately. Blue reads as hospital; the patient of an aesthetic surgeon is not looking for a hospital, but for care.

        We applied the identity to the surfaces the surgeon really touches: clinic signage, surgical mask, scrubs, embossed business card and digital icon variants. The emblem was drawn to stand alone — it still reads at profile-picture size.

    - heading: Social Media
      text: |-
        We built the content on the questions patients genuinely ask: will surgery leave a scar, does botox freeze your expression, who is a breast reduction actually for. Procedures like a mommy makeover are covered as a process, not as a promised result.

        The visual language avoids the dramatic before-and-after. In aesthetic surgery that format is the easiest route and the most expensive one: it draws attention in the short term and, over time, turns the surgeon into someone who sells operations.

        Occasion posts follow the same measured tone — the account does not become a different brand on a public holiday.
      images:
        - '/src/assets/images/projects/op-dr-ismail-buyukcayir/sosyal-medya.webp'
    - heading: Video Production
      text: |-
        In aesthetic surgery trust is built by the surgeon, not by the visuals. So the videos are built on him speaking, rather than on stock footage or animation.

        Each film answers a single question, and asks that question openly in the title. Can you give birth after a tummy tuck, who is a breast reduction suitable for, what people get wrong about aesthetic surgery — all questions patients ask before an operation and then search for online.

        Filming, editing and subtitling are ours. Anyone watching with the sound off can still follow; on social media that is how most views begin.
